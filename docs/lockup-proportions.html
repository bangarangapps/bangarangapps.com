Bangarang · lockup review · October 2026 · shipped 14 Oct

The sail,
measured

Every number here is measured from the shipped files: cap 700 units, stem 150, mark 910 tall by 792 wide. The sail is already sized to the system. What is not quite right is the air around it.

Leave

Scale. The mark is 910 units tall = 6.07 stems = 18.2u on a 50-unit module, hung exactly centred on the cap band (105 above, 106 below). Its ink area is 108% of the average letter, so it carries the same colour on the line as G or N.

Leave

Left/right balance. The mark's mass sits 74 units right of its box centre, so it needs more air on its left than its right. Current optical gaps run 275 : 224 = 1.23. The typeface's own A runs 220 : 182 = 1.21. Same asymmetry; the pads are doing the right job.

Change

Overall looseness. Both gaps are about 25% and 23% wider than the A the sail replaces, and the gap before the mark (275) is the widest in the whole word (next widest: 207). Tightening to +10% over the A's spacing closes the rhythm without crowding.

Optional

Snap the mark to exactly 18u (6 stems, 2u overhang). It is a 1.2% change nobody will see, but it makes the system describable: cap 14u, stem 3u, mark 18u, overhang 2u.

01 · Blocks on the lockup

Cap, stem,
overhang

Module u = 50 units. The cap is 14u, the stem 3u, the mark 18.2u, the overhang 2.1u each side (red blocks). The shaded red areas are the white space the eye counts between the mark and its neighbours, measured row by row to a depth of 175 units.

What the blocks say

  • Overhang above equals overhang below (105 / 106), so the mark is centred on the caps. Correct, and it stays correct at every size.
  • The sail is 1.22x the width of the A it replaces (792 vs 651). Wider and taller than a letter is what makes it read as a symbol and not a misshapen A.
  • Nearest bounding-box clearance: 72 to G, 149 to R. Letter pairs in this word sit at 40 to 120.

How to read it

  • Optical gap = mean horizontal white between two shapes across the cap band, each shape's profile clamped to 175 units from its edge so deep counters count less. It is the standard area method for kerning, applied to the sail.
  • Closest approach at equal height (not bounding box): G to sail 152, sail to R 124. G to A is 119 and A to R is 72 in the plain word.
02 · Blocks on the mark

Inside
the sail

The mark's bounding box is 5.28 x 6.07 stems. The grid lines are one stem apart. The three pieces stand in a measured sequence, and the centre of mass sits right of the box centre, which is why the pads are asymmetric.

Findings

  • Piece heights 232 : 376 : 562 = 1 : 1.62 : 2.42. The first step is a golden step (1.62). The artwork has a real rhythm; do not touch the pieces.
  • Apex is 20 units right of the box centre; centre of mass 74 right and 22 low. The A it replaces has its centre of mass 32 low, the sail's is 22 low: the same low centre of gravity.
  • Edge angle 51.5 deg from horizontal against the A's 71 deg. The sail leans harder than any letter. That lean is what stops it reading as an A, so it is not a flaw to correct.

Weight match

  • Ink area 276,146 vs G 276,477, N 281,428, B 262,966, A 207,522. The sail is as dark as the heavy letters; the A it replaces is the lightest letter in the word.
  • Stroke thickness inside the sail averages 225 units against the stem's 150. It is a bolder shape than the type around it, which is what lets it stand taller without feeling thin.
